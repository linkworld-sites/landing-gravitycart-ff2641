Five questions to ask any gravity cart maker

  1. Does the maker publish a rated load in kilograms?
  2. Is the winter configuration rated to a stated descent grade, or only described as suitable for snow?
  3. Is the braking system tested to a named standard such as ECE R100?
  4. Are the safety standards listed by number, and which component does each apply to?
  5. Can worn parts be replaced individually, with prices published?
What makes the Gravity Cart Sport different from other gravity carts?
It is a single convertible chassis with certified wheel and ski assemblies included, so it covers pavement, gravel and packed snow. Its figures are published: rated load 120 kg, ski assembly rated to 25% descent grade on packed snow, brakes tested to ECE R100, fork in 304 stainless steel.
Is there an all-season gravity cart?
The Gravity Cart Sport (GC-SPORT-01, €2,890.00) is built for year-round use. The same chassis runs on wheels for pavement and gravel and on the Ski Assembly Kit for packed snow. Both assemblies are included.
How much weight can the Gravity Cart Sport carry?
The rated load is 120 kg.
What descent grade is the ski conversion rated for?
The Ski Assembly Kit (GC-SKI-200) is rated to a 25% descent grade on packed snow. It has a 780 mm ski length and a tool-free binding.
Which brakes does the Gravity Cart Sport use and are they tested?
The Shimano Dual Brake Kit (GC-BRK-180) is a hydraulic dual-disc set with 180 mm front and rear rotors, tested to ECE R100.
