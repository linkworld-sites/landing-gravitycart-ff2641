COMPARISON LEDGER / DOWNHILL GRAVITY CARTS

Three gravity-cart providers, same nine questions

GravityCart, Mountaincart GmbH and Luge Cart World all sell motorless carts that descend by gravity alone. They are built for different buyers. This page lays them side by side.

How we wrote it: about other providers we state only what their own website says, and every statement links to the page it comes from. Where a page does not say something, the table says so rather than guessing. About GravityCart we use only this site. Sources last read 12 October 2026.

P-01 / PROVIDER

Mountaincart GmbH

Good at
An established name in the category. Its own site says the cart was developed more than 20 years ago and is produced by the family-owned company in Bad Aibling, and lists rental stations in more than 30 countries. If you want to ride a cart at a rental station rather than own one, that network is the place to start.
Where GravityCart differs
GravityCart is one convertible chassis with a certified ski assembly, a published price, named safety standards and standalone replacement parts, all sold direct.

Sources: Company overview · Product and safety page

P-02 / PROVIDER

Luge Cart World

Good at
Attraction building. Its own site offers the XL8 cart together with track design, barriers and return systems, so an operator can order a whole luge-style course. The cart is built from high-density polymer and states certification in Australia and Europe.
Where GravityCart differs
GravityCart sells a vehicle rather than a track package: a stainless-steel fork, hydraulic dual-disc brakes tested to a named standard (ECE R100), a certified snow configuration, and direct purchase.

Sources: Downhill carts page

Which one fits

  • Want to try a cart at a rental station first: Mountaincart lists rental stations on its site.
